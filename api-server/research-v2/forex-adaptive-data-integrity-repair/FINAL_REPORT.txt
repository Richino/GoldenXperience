Two findings emerged from the repair that matter more than the repair itself.

  (a) THE ENTIRE MULTI-STRATEGY BOOK IS GROSS-POSITIVE AND NET-NEGATIVE.
      Now that cost is expressed in R for the first time:

          gross  +0.93R      cost  7.58R      net  -6.65R

      Across 55 trades the strategies picked directions that were very slightly
      better than a coin flip, and the spread took eight times the edge. This is
      the live-book confirmation of what the earlier 10,009-trade replay
      indicated. Breakout is the sharpest case: +3.200R gross, -0.159R net.

  (b) THE CORRUPTION WAS ALREADY CHANGING WHAT THE ENGINE DID.
      EMA's evidence bucket held 51 observations before the repair and holds 41
      after. The learning threshold is 50. EMA had been promoted out of
      COLLECTING into LEARNING on evidence that was 20% duplicates of itself,
      and the repair correctly puts it back. This was not a reporting problem.

Sample size remains far too small for active adaptation — the deepest exact
context bucket holds 3 observations against a requirement of 100 — so the
correct next step is to keep collecting, now on clean evidence.


================================================================================
1. WHY THE OLD ROWS BECAME INCONSISTENT
================================================================================

Traced through the real execution path before anything was changed:

  collectMultiStrategyCycle()          paper-cycle.ts
    → persistPaperEvaluation()         writes the candidate row, execution_status
                                       computed BEFORE execution is attempted:
                                       the engine's own pick is written 'blocked'
                                       on purpose, so a selection the risk gates
                                       turn away stays honestly blocked
    → applyMomentumInversion()
    → openPaperTrade()                 INSERT INTO paper_strategy_trades
        └─ UPDATE paper_strategy_evaluations
             SET trade_created=true, paper_trade_id=…, execution_status='selected'
             WHERE strategy_version_id=… AND instrument=… AND decision_time=…
    → resolveShadowCandidates()        labels execution_status IN ('suppressed','blocked')
    → loadAdaptiveEvidence()           reads trades UNION shadow outcomes

THE DEFECT. The promotion to 'selected' is an UPDATE matched on three columns.
In the original ordering the evaluation row was written AFTER openPaperTrade, so
that UPDATE matched ZERO ROWS — and an UPDATE that matches nothing is not an
error. The trade existed; the evaluation kept saying it never happened.

Two consequences chained off it:

  1. The candidate stayed filed as 'blocked', which is precisely the set
     resolveShadowCandidates() labels. So it computed a HYPOTHETICAL outcome for
     an opportunity that had really been traded.
  2. loadAdaptiveEvidence() reads both tables and merges them into the same
     context bucket. The same opportunity was therefore counted twice — once
     with its actual result, once with a simulated one.

Measured before the repair: 201 observations, of which 48 were duplicates (24%),
and 5 of those duplicate pairs DISAGREED with each other about the outcome.

WAS THE FORWARD FIX ALREADY WORKING? Partly, and it was verified rather than
assumed. Two source-level fixes were already present:

  · the write order had been corrected (persistPaperEvaluation now runs before
    openPaperTrade), and
  · EXECUTION_STATUS_CONFLICT_RULE stops a later tick inside the same M15 bar
    overwriting a 'selected' back to 'blocked'.

Both are real and both are covered by the existing scripts/test-evaluation-upsert.ts.
But they were insufficient, for a reason the repair had to address:

  · They protect NEW rows only. The 48 damaged rows were never corrected and
    were still corrupting evidence on every single cycle.
  · The correctness of "did this trade?" still rested on a three-column UPDATE
    matching. Nothing structurally prevented it silently matching zero rows
    again — which is exactly how the defect arose, undetected, for days, at an
    87% failure rate.
  · resolveShadowCandidates() and loadAdaptiveEvidence() both still trusted
    execution_status as the test for "not executed". A status column that had
    already proven capable of drifting was the sole guard.

BEHAVIOUR THAT CHANGED, AND WHY IT HAD TO

  Exactly one live-path behaviour changed, and it is a recording change, not a
  decision change. `evaluateHardGates` used to collapse "the calendar is
  unusable" and "a high-impact release is inside the buffer" into the same
  recorded value, because `newsClear` is false either way. It now emits four
  distinct states. The gate's PASS/FAIL is byte-for-byte identical — it still
  fails closed on an unusable calendar — and that is asserted by test. What
  changed is only what gets written down.

  Everything else in the live path is additive: extra columns written at insert
  and on close, extra research rows, extra exclusions in a read query.

3. THE FIVE REPAIRS
================================================================================

── 3.1  EXECUTED TRADES FILED AS BLOCKED SHADOW CANDIDATES ────────────────────

FORWARD FIX (verified, not assumed)

  A. `openPaperTrade` now resolves the evaluation id BEFORE the insert and
     writes it onto the trade, backed by a UNIQUE index. "Was this opportunity
     executed?" is now a foreign key, not the residue of an UPDATE.
  B. `resolveShadowCandidates` no longer trusts execution_status. It disqualifies
     a candidate four independent ways — the evaluation backs a trade
     (evaluation_id), names one (paper_trade_id), is flagged (trade_created), or
     a trade exists on the same (instrument, bar, family). Any one is enough, so
     drift in a single marker cannot resurrect the defect.
  C. `loadAdaptiveEvidence` applies the same four exclusions at read time.

  Verified by test: `test-evidence-integrity-db.ts` §1 asserts zero evaluations
  that produced a trade remain filed as blocked, and zero live shadow outcomes
  belong to an executed opportunity.

HISTORICAL REPAIR

  Matching is on (strategy_version_id, instrument, decision_time). That triple
  carries a UNIQUE constraint on BOTH tables, so at most one row on each side can
  satisfy it — the association is a proven 1:1, not a heuristic. Anything that
  could not be matched that way was counted and left untouched.

    trades linked to their evaluation                 0  →  66
    evaluations restamped trade_created               18 →  66
    evaluations restamped execution_status='selected' 1  →  55
    shadow outcomes superseded                        0  →  48
    trades left unlinked (no evaluation row exists)   18   (all legacy)

  The 48 stale shadow rows were MARKED, not deleted. Each carries
  superseded_by_trade_id, superseded_at and a reason. Deleting them would have
  destroyed the evidence that the defect existed and that it was repaired.

DEDUPLICATION

  ONE ADAPTIVE OBSERVATION = one strategy ARM at one OPPORTUNITY:

      experiment · family · config_version · instrument · decision_time · strategy_direction

  · strategy_direction is COALESCE(original_direction, direction) — what the
    STRATEGY concluded, not what an execution policy traded, because that is the
    key the engine looks a candidate up by on the next bar.
  · config_version is part of the identity: a parameter change is a different
    strategy and must not be averaged into one bucket.
  · decision_time (the completed M15 bar), not opened_at: the collector runs far
    more often than every fifteen minutes and every tick inside one bar is the
    same opportunity.
  · The rule that follows: an opportunity contributes EITHER its executed result
    OR its hypothetical one. Never both.

  Two independent measurements are reported, deliberately:

    observations IN THE TABLES (no read-time reconstruction)  201 → 153
      duplicate opportunities                                  48 → 0
      conflicting outcomes                                       5 → 0
    observations the LOADER counts                            201 → 153
      duplicate opportunities                                  48 → 0
      conflicting outcomes                                       5 → 0

  Both are shown because a guard in the SELECT and a repair in the rows fix the
  same defect from opposite ends. Reporting only the loader's view would let a
  corrupt table look clean behind a careful query. The two agreeing at 153 is
  the proof that the ROWS are clean.

── 3.2  PAIRED ORIGINAL vs INVERTED MOMENTUM ──────────────────────────────────

  The problem: `applyMomentumInversion` REPLACES the direction, so exactly one
  arm is ever traded, and the arm that was not traded got no result at all —
  shadow resolution only labels suppressed/blocked candidates, and the executing
  path promotes the row to 'selected'. The counterfactual, which is the entire
  point of the experiment, was never being recorded.

  `momentum_inversion_arms` holds two rows per eligible opportunity under one
  deterministic pair_id (md5 of cohort|instrument|decision_time, so re-recording
  the same bar lands on the same pair and the write is idempotent):

    · exactly one real execution at most — enforced by a partial UNIQUE index,
      not by the caller remembering to check
    · the selected arm takes its outcome from the real trade, verbatim, and is
      never re-simulated
    · the unselected arm is resolved by `resolveShadowOutcome`, the same pure
      labeller the shadow candidates use, which returns null until the outcome
      would genuinely have been known in real time
    · outcome_source ('executed' | 'shadow') records which, so an actual result
      and a hypothetical one can never be silently interchanged
    · both arms share instrument, bar, config version, regime, ATR and SPREAD,
      and carry identical stop/target DISTANCES — direction is the only
      independent variable
    · each arm fills its OWN side of the book (long at the ask, short at the
      bid). Negating the original instead would hand the counterfactual a free
      round trip and manufacture an edge that does not exist.
    · the table is deliberately NOT read by loadAdaptiveEvidence, exactly like
      momentum_short_inversion_pairs, so a research record cannot influence live
      selection or double-count the executed arm

  Result: 25 pairs, 25 fully resolved, 25 with exactly one real execution,
  0 with more than one.

    cohort momentum-inversion-backfill-v1     arms  resolved  executed   net R
      original                                  25        25        25  -7.454
      inverted                                  25        25         0  +6.973

  ON THE COHORT NAME — this matters. Historical pairs are filed under
  `momentum-inversion-backfill-v1`, NOT `momentum-inversion-v1`. The forward
  experiment activated 2026-08-23T21:00:24Z and the last Momentum trade opened
  2026-08-21, so every historical trade predates it. Pooling them would let the
  sample that GENERATED the inversion hypothesis also appear to CONFIRM it —
  the exact contamination momentum_short_inversion_pairs was built to avoid.

  So the -7.454R vs +6.973R above is NOT evidence that inversion works. It is
  the discovery sample, now properly paired and properly quarantined. Against it
  stands an 11,977-signal historical replay that found no inversion effect at
  all (-0.0600 inverted vs -0.0595 original). The question stays open, and the
  forward cohort still holds zero rows because no valid Momentum SHORT candidate
  has fired since activation.

  Reconstructing the opposite side is arithmetic, not a guess: `entry` is by
  construction the executable side of the book and `spread_pips` is the spread
  at that same instant, so the other side is entry ∓ spread — exact, from two
  stored values.

── 3.3  NEWS CONTEXT PERSISTENCE ──────────────────────────────────────────────

  Traced: the gate runs in `evaluateHardGates`; its verdict lands in the
  `conditions` array; `openPaperTrade` reads `setup.features.newsStatus` for the
  `news_status` column. But none of the four Phase-2 strategies ever set
  `newsStatus` on their features literal, so the column read 'not_evaluated' on
  all 55 multi-strategy trades while the conditions array beside it said 'clear'
  on all 55.

  Fixed at the single point where it cannot drift: `finalizeCandidate` derives
  `newsStatus` FROM the condition list, so the column and the conditions come
  from the same fact. No strategy has to remember to do it, and a future family
  gets it for free.

  Also fixed: the gate now distinguishes `calendar_unavailable` from
  `high_impact`. Both used to be written as "high impact" because `newsClear`
  collapses to false either way — so a trade paused for a MISSING calendar was
  indistinguishable from one paused for an actual release.

    news_status = 'not_evaluated'      73 → 18   (the 18 are genuinely so:
                                                 14 legacy rows whose condition
                                                 says not evaluated, 4 with no
                                                 recoverable News condition)
    multi-strategy trades recording a real verdict   0 → 55

  Structured news context preserved per trade: news_status (gate verdict),
  news_impact_tag, news_evaluation_state, news_currency, news_event_name,
  news_event_time, news_minutes_from_news, news_impact_level,
  news_matched_event_ids, news_calendar_events_nearby, news_window_config,
  news_tagged_at. Every NEAR/HIGH_IMPACT tag is asserted by test to carry
  currency, event name, event time, minutes-from-event and impact level.

  Nothing was fabricated. Where no classification ran, the state stays
  NOT_EVALUATED; no unknown row was converted to NO_NEWS.

── 3.4  MISLEADING LEGACY NO_NEWS ─────────────────────────────────────────────

  22 trades were tagged NO_NEWS while no stored calendar event existed within a
  day of them. That is an absence of DATA, not an absence of NEWS.

  Four states are now distinguished, using the existing tag vocabulary so every
  historical value stays valid and every existing reader keeps working:

    NO_NEWS                     confirmed quiet — REQUIRES calendar coverage
    NEAR_NEWS / HIGH_IMPACT_NEWS   news present (the two existing severities)
    INSUFFICIENT_CALENDAR_DATA  a verdict was computed, the calendar cannot support it
    NOT_EVALUATED               no classification was ever run

  The downgrade rule: a NO_NEWS verdict with zero calendar coverage becomes
  INSUFFICIENT_CALENDAR_DATA. A positive match is NEVER downgraded — finding an
  event proves coverage by itself. Unknown coverage fails towards
  INSUFFICIENT_CALENDAR_DATA rather than towards confirmed quiet.

    NO_NEWS without calendar coverage    22 → 0
    tagged INSUFFICIENT_CALENDAR_DATA     0 → 22
    news_evaluation_state recorded        0 → 84

  Idempotent, and self-correcting: `backfillNewsTags` now also retries rows
  parked as INSUFFICIENT_CALENDAR_DATA, so a trade whose calendar later fills in
  is upgraded rather than frozen at "no data" forever.

── 3.5  TRANSACTION COST IN R ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  WHAT THE CODE ACTUALLY DOES, established before assuming a bug. `buildTradePlan`
  takes entry on the EXECUTABLE side (ask for a long, bid for a short), and
  `labelOutcome` resolves a long against bidHigh/bidLow and a short against
  askLow/askHigh — the exit is measured on the OPPOSITE side of the book from the
  entry. Mid-to-mid that round trip costs exactly one full spread.

  So the stored result_r was ALREADY net of spread. The obvious "fix" —
  subtracting a spread cost from result_r — would have charged the spread twice
  and invented a loss that was never paid. The decomposition therefore runs the
  other way:

      net_result_r    = result_r                  (already net; asserted equal)
      spread_cost_r   = spread_pips · pipSize / |entry − stop|
      total_cost_r    = spread_cost_r  (+ commission + slippage where known)
      gross_result_r  = net_result_r + total_cost_r
      identity        net_result_r = gross_result_r − total_cost_r   ✓ all rows

  Coverage:
    trades with spread_cost_r / net / gross          0 → 84  (100%)
    shadow outcomes with a cost decomposition        0 → 146 (100%)
    trades with cost_basis 'unknown'                       0
    trades not calculable                                  0

  NOT FABRICATED. `commission_cost_r` and `slippage_cost_r` are NULL on every
  row and stay NULL. OANDA's trade endpoint reports neither, and no
  modelled-versus-filled pair is stored anywhere, so any number would be
  invented. NULL means unknown, never zero, and `cost_basis='spread_only'` says
  exactly what the total contains. `result_basis` additionally records whether
  result_r came from a broker close (carrying real exit slippage) or a modelled
  one, so two different measurement regimes are no longer pooled unmarked.

  WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY:

    family      n   avg cost   max cost    gross R     net R
    breakout   18    0.1866R    0.5012R      3.200    -0.159
    ema        12    0.1699R    0.3673R      3.002     0.964
    momentum   25    0.0873R    0.1599R     -5.272    -7.454
    legacy     29    0.1605R    0.3602R     -0.786    -5.442

    multi-strategy total:   gross +0.93R   cost 7.58R   net -6.65R

  Breakout's entire apparent edge is the spread. EMA keeps 32% of its gross. The
  book as a whole is gross-positive and net-negative by a factor of eight. A
  selector ranking on gross would have preferred breakout — the family whose
  tight stops make the same spread the largest fraction of R.

4. MAKING ADAPTIVE EVIDENCE NET-OF-COST
================================================================================

  `BucketStat` now carries `netR` AND `grossR`. `expectancy()` — the function
  `decideInstrument` ranks and suppresses on — divides netR. `grossExpectancy()`
  exists for research and is read by nothing in the decision path.

  Both loader queries were changed to read the net column with an explicit
  fallback chain that can never substitute a gross figure for a net one:

    executed:  COALESCE(net_result_r, result_r)
    shadow:    COALESCE(s.net_result_r, s.result_r)
    gross:     COALESCE(gross_result_r, net_result_r, result_r)

  The gross fallback ends at the NET value rather than at zero, so an
  un-backfilled row understates the cost gap instead of inventing a free trade.

  What the engine now sees, per family bucket:

    family      resolved   net E[R]   gross E[R]   state
    ema               41     0.2316       0.4114   collecting
    breakout          56     0.0794       0.2502   learning
    momentum          51     0.0596       0.1464   learning
    meanrev            5     0.6955       0.8534   collecting

  Gross expectancy is 2–3x net across the board. Ranking on gross would have
  been ranking on setup quality; ranking on net is ranking on what the account
  keeps.
